## Overview

Large language models are increasingly used to generate research summaries, answer questions, retrieve information, and produce scholarly references. Although these systems can accelerate literature discovery and research writing, they can also generate fabricated citations, incorrect bibliographic details, unsupported claims, and references that do not actually justify the statements for which they are provided. These problems become particularly important when language models are combined with web search, retrieval-augmented generation, or agentic systems that present external sources as evidence.

This repository focuses on citation integrity in large language model and search-augmented systems. It brings together verified research papers, datasets, tools, open-source implementations, and learning resources related to citation fabrication, citation verification, hallucination detection, factuality evaluation, retrieval-augmented generation, and evidence-grounded generation. The collection also covers methods for checking whether references exist, whether bibliographic metadata is accurate, and whether cited sources genuinely support generated claims.

The repository is intended as a reusable research resource for studying the reliability of AI-generated scholarly information. It connects the earlier AI-assisted research paper and citation-integrity audit with verified external literature and practical resources that can support future experiments and benchmarking studies.

The collection is particularly focused on:

- Citation fabrication and nonexistent references
- Citation correctness and source attribution
- Verification of claims against cited evidence
- Hallucination detection and evaluation
- Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
- Search-augmented and generative search systems
- Evaluation and benchmarking of LLM-generated information

## Research Themes

### Citation Fabrication

Research investigating references that are nonexistent, incorrectly attributed, inaccurate, or otherwise fabricated by language models.

### Citation Verification

Methods for determining whether a generated citation exists and whether the cited source actually supports the claim or surrounding citation context.

### Hallucination Detection

Benchmarks and methods for detecting unsupported or factually incorrect content generated by large language models.

### Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Research on grounding language models using retrieved evidence and evaluating whether generated responses remain faithful to that evidence.

### Generative and Search-Augmented Systems

Research on systems that combine language models with web search or other external information sources, including evaluation of source quality and citation verifiability.

## Verification Principles

Because this repository focuses on citation integrity, resources are selected with an emphasis on verifiability.

For research papers, important bibliographic information such as the title, authors, publication year, venue, and DOI or persistent identifier is checked against authoritative scholarly records where available.

For datasets and implementations, the repository records the official source or project link and explains how the resource relates to citation integrity, hallucination, retrieval, or evidence-grounded generation.

Preprints are explicitly identified as preprints rather than being presented as peer-reviewed publications.

## Research Project

This repository was developed around the research topic:

> **Benchmarking Citation Fabrication Rates Across Large Language Models and Agentic Search Tools**

The project focuses on how reliably different AI systems produce scholarly references and on methods for measuring and comparing citation fabrication and citation-support errors.
